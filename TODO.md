### Editor: camera alignment

- [x] **Hidden corners in an outdoor camera's view.** The editor's camera view offers every corner of the house outline as a handle to drag (`planCorners(..., outline)` in `_renderCameraView()`), including the ones the house hides from the camera (the back corners). Leave out the corners whose line of sight from the camera crosses the indoor rooms.
- [x] **Only the facing facades for an outdoor camera.** For an outdoor camera, the camera view (`_renderCameraView()`) draws the lines of every room (`planLines(plan.rooms, …)`), back walls included, which clutters the picture. Draw only the outer walls facing the camera (outward normal towards the camera position, as for the projection in `_buildScene()`), and the ground lines in front of them.
- [x] **Correct the plan's proportions.** When the drawn plan is a little off (a wall measured too short), pinned corners can't all fall on their place in the picture. Let the camera view adjust the gap between two corners horizontally and between two corners vertically (a stretch along the plan's width / depth, and of the wall height) to make up for these measuring errors. This only changes this camera's projection (a correction stored in its settings, applied when its picture is projected), never the plan: the picture is warped to fit the 3D model as well as possible.
  - [x] **Wide-angle lens distortion (seen on a real camera).** The main cause in practice is not the plan but the lens: real outdoor cameras have a wide-angle lens with strong barrel distortion (straight facade edges, gutters and the roof line come out curved, bulging out from the picture's center). The camera model is a pinhole without distortion (`cameraPose()` / `toPicture()`, and the `uMode == 5` branch of `GL_FRAGMENT`), so no direction / tilt / fov / height can make the pinned corners all fit: the house projected in 3D shows the picture's facade smeared over the roof and ground, and the edges don't line up. A stretch of the plan can't fix this; the projection has to undistort the picture.
    - Add a radial distortion to the camera model: picture point = center + r·(1 + k1·r² + k2·r⁴) around the optical center (Brown–Conrady, `k1 < 0` for barrel; maybe a fisheye equidistant model `r = f·θ` as an alternative for very wide lenses, > 120°). Store `k1` (and `k2`) in the camera's settings, `0` by default (today's behavior).
    - Apply it everywhere a world point goes to the picture: `toPicture()` (hence `segmentToPicture()` and the editor's lines and corners, which then come out curved like in the picture: draw long segments as polylines), and the shader's projection (distort `p` before the texture lookup, same formula, with `k1` / `k2` uniforms). The shadow map (`GL_DEPTH_FRAGMENT`) stays in undistorted coordinates as long as both sides agree.
    - Fit it: add `k1` (then `k2`) as free parameters of `solveCamera()` once there are enough pinned corners (≥ 5–6), with a pull towards 0 so few points don't produce a wild curve; also a manual slider "Lens distortion" in the camera view to adjust it by eye on the curved lines.
    - Done with the division model (`distortion`, one parameter, never folding back, closed form both ways, covers fisheye-like fields) rather than Brown–Conrady k1 / k2. The plan's correction also got a shift (`shift_x`, `shift_y`): a stretch alone around the plan's middle moves the house too.
    - Testing: the fake cameras ([camera.py](dev/ha-config/custom_components/plooum_dev/camera.py)) are perfect pinholes; give one (e.g. `camera.garden_camera`, or a new one) a known barrel distortion so the fit can be checked against the known `k1`.
- [x] **Free corners: simpler alignment (outdoor cameras first).** Lining a camera up is laborious: every pinned corner re-solves the whole camera model (`solveCamera()`), so the corners already placed move on their own, and the model never fits reality (plan errors, wide-angle lens), hence too many settings (lens distortion slider, "Correct the plan's proportions", 5 / 6 pin thresholds, error in px). Rework it:
  - **A pinned corner stays exactly where it is dropped**, and nothing else moves. No order, no minimum count.
  - **Only the most visible facade's 4 corners** are offered as handles for an outdoor camera (the outer wall facing the camera the most, largest in the picture); don't show the corners of facades that barely show. These 4 corners are enough.
  - **Projection by a warp**: the camera model (pose from the plan + direction, refit quietly from the pinned corners, never moving them) only gives a first guess, places the unpinned points and keeps computing what the house hides (shadow map). On top of it, the picture is warped so that each pinned corner falls exactly on its place: each pin's offset (where the model puts it → where it was pinned) is interpolated smoothly between the pins (RBF / mesh warp), in `toPicture()` and in the `uMode == 5` branch of `GL_FRAGMENT` (pins as a uniform array, ~16 max). Slightly bent straight lines between badly placed pins are acceptable.
  - **Fewer settings**: drop the lens distortion slider, "Correct the plan's proportions", the pin thresholds and the error shown in px from the camera view; the warp absorbs plan and lens errors. Keep reading the existing `distortion` / `stretch_*` / `shift_*` settings so current configs still work.
  - **Magnifier while dragging**: a loupe showing the picture zoomed around the dragged corner (offset above the finger), since on a phone the finger hides the very corner being aimed at.
  - **Feedback on the dragged corner**: highlight the corner being dragged in the camera view and on the plan (a small plan view or the editor's plan), so it's clear which corner of the house is being placed.
- [ ] **Picture aspect correction.** Not done with the lens distortion: a camera whose stream is scaled to another ratio (or has non-square pixels) shows the plan squeezed horizontally. Add a picture-wide horizontal scale to the camera model (`toPicture()`, the `uMode == 5` branch of `GL_FRAGMENT`), fitted by `solveCamera()` like the distortion, pulled towards 1.
- [ ] **Fisheye lenses.** The division model (`distortion`) fits wide-angle lenses up to ~150°; a true fisheye (equidistant, `r = f·θ`) past 180° can't be shown by a pinhole-based model. Only if a real camera needs it.

### Projection and 3D view

- [x] **Roof over narrow strips.** A part of a floor that a floor above doesn't cover gets its own hip roof (`roofRects()` + `hipRoof()`): a strip along an upper wall looks like a ledge. A shed roof leaning on that wall would look more natural.
- [x] **Frozen projected picture.** Projected pictures are snapshots reloaded every `refresh_interval` (3 s by default): if the camera is turned (PTZ, knocked), a fresh picture no longer matches the alignment and is projected askew. Add a "Capture" button to the editor's camera view: it uploads the current picture through Home Assistant's image upload (as the picture card does; check that `image_upload` is available) and stores its id in the camera's settings as the alignment's reference picture. The camera view aligns on that reference, and a new option `projection_picture: frozen` (default) always projects it, never reloaded. Without a reference yet, keep today's behavior.
- [x] **Watched live projection.** Depends on the frozen picture. `projection_picture: live`: project a recent snapshot (own, long interval, e.g. 5 min, separate from the screens' `refresh_interval`), but only while it still looks like the reference: compare both pictures scaled down (~64 px) on their edges rather than their colors, to tolerate light changes. A snapshot that differs too much, fails to load or comes out black never replaces the last good one (or the reference), and the camera shows "Camera moved? Re-align it" (editor and its screen). Expect infrared night pictures to be rejected: keeping the last daytime picture is fine.
- [x] **Fade the screen of a projecting camera.** In 3D, when a camera projects its picture onto the scene, its screen in front of it hides part of that projection and repeats it. Make it much less visible (semi-transparent, or smaller) for cameras with `projection`.
